# Interface craft reference

Load this reference for visual-system work, a new customer-facing surface, redesign, or polish. Treat these as decision prompts, not a universal aesthetic.

## Register

### Brand surfaces

Marketing sites, campaigns, portfolios, and launches may use stronger composition, art direction, narrative pacing, and memorable motion because identity is part of the value. Keep conversion paths obvious and accessibility intact.

### Product surfaces

Apps, dashboards, admin tools, and settings should optimize repeated use, information retrieval, stable placement, predictable controls, and low cognitive overhead. Character should reinforce the domain without competing with work.

## System decisions

- **Typography:** choose a legible body face and a purposeful display strategy. Pair fonts by contrast, not near-duplication. Establish a small type scale, suitable line height, and readable measure; keep prose near 45–75 characters per line.
- **Color:** choose a strategy before individual values: restrained accent, committed field, role-based palette, or color-dominant surface. Derive neutrals from the brand rather than defaulting to fashionable cream, blue-black, or purple. Verify contrast numerically.
- **Layout:** establish containers, gutters, alignment anchors, spacing rhythm, and density. Use grouping and whitespace before adding cards. Avoid nesting containers merely to create visual interest.
- **Shape:** define a restrained radius, border, elevation, and icon language. Pills belong to tags and compact actions, not every container.
- **Imagery:** prefer real product artifacts, licensed photography, diagrams, or deliberate generated assets. Do not substitute crude illustrative SVGs or generic stock imagery for missing direction.
- **Motion:** define what motion communicates: continuity, hierarchy, feedback, or delight. Animate transform and opacity by default, never hide essential content behind a reveal, and provide reduced-motion behavior.
- **Writing:** labels should describe actions and consequences. Remove vague eyebrows, slogans, and section copy that could move to a competitor unchanged.

## Common reflexes to challenge

Challenge a pattern when content, task, or brand does not justify it:

- purple or blue gradients used as identity by default;
- glass panels, dark glows, or decorative grid backgrounds;
- a vague oversized hero followed by uniform rounded cards;
- icon tile plus heading plus paragraph repeated across every section;
- tiny uppercase labels or numbered markers above every heading;
- gradients clipped into text;
- fake metrics, ornamental charts, and testimonial placeholders;
- excessively soft shadows paired with borders;
- extreme radii on ordinary sections and controls;
- identical entrance animation on every section;
- motion, 3D, grain, or illustration added because the page feels empty.

Do not replace these reflexes with another fashionable family. Derive distinction from the user's situation, content, product artifacts, and brand history.

## Preflight

Before implementation, confirm:

- the page or flow has one dominant task or argument;
- the hierarchy works as a plain content outline;
- every section earns its place;
- tokens can express the direction consistently;
- the chosen assets actually exist and may be used;
- the design remains credible with real copy and awkward data;
- the direction is recognizably specific without depending on novelty.
