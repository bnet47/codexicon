# Campaigns and channels reference

Use for launches, lifecycle email, social, paid media, partnerships, public relations, and coordinated campaigns.

## Campaign spine

Define one campaign spine before producing assets:

- objective and audience;
- trigger or timely reason;
- central concept and promise;
- proof and objection;
- offer and action;
- channel roles and sequence;
- measurement window and stop or change rule.

Each channel should play a distinct role. Do not paste the same copy everywhere.

## Channel adaptation

- **Landing destination:** preserve message match with the source; complete the argument and action.
- **Email:** earn the subject line, make the sender and purpose clear, provide a plain route to the action, and respect consent and unsubscribe requirements.
- **Social:** create a self-contained useful idea; match platform format without imitating engagement bait.
- **Paid media:** align creative, audience, claim, destination, and tracking. Verify current platform specifications and restricted-category rules before production.
- **PR:** lead with a genuinely newsworthy change and verifiable relevance; separate editorial outreach from advertising.
- **Partners:** define mutual audience value, responsibilities, approvals, data handling, and attribution.

## Sequence

Map what the audience knows and needs next at each touchpoint. Avoid repeating the launch announcement without adding proof, explanation, use cases, objection handling, or a new reason to act.

## Integrity

- Never manufacture scarcity, countdowns, demand, customer enthusiasm, reviews, or earned coverage.
- Do not imply a partnership, endorsement, certification, or comparison without approval and evidence.
- Treat outreach lists, tracking, retargeting, and personalization as privacy and permission decisions.
- Verify links, destinations, dates, offer terms, exclusions, and ownership before activation.

## Production handoff

For every asset record owner, format, dimensions or limits, source material, destination, tracking, approval status, and publication authority. A draft schedule is not authorization to publish.
