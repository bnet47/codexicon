# Research and measurement reference

Use for customer research, positioning, market or competitor analysis, SEO discovery, analytics, experiments, and marketing strategy.

## Evidence order

Prefer evidence closest to real behavior:

1. product usage, transactions, search, conversion, retention, and support data;
2. direct customer interviews and observed tasks;
3. sales, success, support, and community records;
4. verified reviews and market behavior;
5. current primary competitor and platform sources;
6. secondary analysis;
7. hypotheses and intuition.

Do not merge these levels into one confident narrative. Record sample, timeframe, segment, source, and important bias.

## Positioning

Define the audience, high-value situation, current alternative, differentiated capability, resulting value, and proof. Test whether the statement remains true when category jargon and unsupported adjectives are removed.

Competitor work should compare real alternatives and decision criteria, not create hostile caricatures. Date volatile facts and link them to their sources.

## Research synthesis

- Separate observations, interpretations, and recommendations.
- Preserve disagreement and segment differences.
- Count recurring themes only when the sample supports counting.
- Use verbatim customer language sparingly and within source permissions.
- Identify what evidence would disconfirm the recommendation.

## Measurement contract

Define before execution:

- behavior expected to change;
- event and exact success condition;
- primary metric and guardrails;
- eligible population and exclusions;
- baseline and measurement window;
- instrumentation owner and data-quality check;
- decision rule: continue, revise, stop, or investigate.

Avoid vanity metrics detached from the intended behavior. Do not invent benchmark targets when no defensible baseline exists.

## Experiments

State the hypothesis as a causal prediction: changing **X** for **audience Y** should change **behavior Z** because **reason R**. Change one strategic dimension where possible, protect users from harmful variants, and define the decision rule before observing results.

For SEO and discovery work, verify current search and platform guidance from primary sources. Optimize for useful, accurate content and crawlable structure; do not promise rankings or fabricate search volume.
