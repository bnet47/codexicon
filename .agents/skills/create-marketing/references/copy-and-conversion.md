# Copy and conversion reference

Use for landing pages, product pages, offers, forms, onboarding, paywalls, pricing presentation, and conversion work.

## Build the message hierarchy

1. State the audience's situation or desired progress in language they recognize.
2. Explain the product's concrete role and boundary.
3. Show how it works with enough detail to reduce uncertainty.
4. Support the promise with approved evidence.
5. Resolve the highest-friction objection.
6. Offer one proportionate next action.

Do not force every page into this exact order. Match the sequence to awareness, risk, complexity, and traffic source.

## Copy standards

- Prefer concrete nouns, verbs, timeframes, and consequences over adjectives.
- Keep headlines understandable without the subheading.
- Make buttons describe the resulting action or destination.
- Put important limits, requirements, and costs near the claim or action they qualify.
- Use examples to clarify behavior, not simulate customer proof.
- Explain unfamiliar mechanisms; remove explanations users already know.
- Keep tone consistent during errors, validation, consent, cancellation, and payment.

## Conversion diagnosis

Separate possible causes before rewriting:

- wrong or poorly qualified audience;
- weak value or offer;
- unclear message;
- insufficient proof or trust;
- excessive effort or required information;
- technical, accessibility, performance, or mobile friction;
- mismatch between acquisition promise and destination;
- measurement error.

Copy cannot repair a weak offer, broken journey, or untrusted claim. Recommend the smallest test that distinguishes competing causes.

## Forms and actions

- Ask only for information needed at that stage.
- Explain why sensitive or unusual information is required.
- Use persistent labels, clear validation, preserved input, and recovery.
- Match commitment: a high-friction action requires stronger clarity and proof.
- Avoid preselected consent, hidden conditions, confirm-shaming, and misleading button hierarchy.

## Variants

Change one strategic dimension per variant: audience framing, problem framing, promise, proof, objection, offer, or action. Do not present synonym swaps as experiments.
