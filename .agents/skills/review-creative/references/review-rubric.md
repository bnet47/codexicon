# Creative review rubric

Load only the sections relevant to the artifact. Use questions as prompts for evidence, not a checklist that forces findings.

## Product truth and trust

- Does the work accurately represent real behavior, limits, availability, pricing, proof, and outcomes?
- Are factual claims sourced, dated when volatile, appropriately qualified, and consistent?
- Are testimonials, logos, metrics, awards, comparisons, and endorsements real and approved?
- Are consent, privacy, safety, accessibility, and material terms understandable at the point of action?
- Does the work avoid deceptive urgency, hidden conditions, confirm-shaming, disguised advertising, and manipulative defaults?

## Interface and experience

### Comprehension and flow

- Is the primary task immediately identifiable?
- Does information appear when it is needed, in an order that supports decisions?
- Are navigation, location, state, consequences, and progress observable?
- Can users reverse, cancel, retry, or safely exit where appropriate?
- Are empty, loading, error, success, stale, offline, and permission states complete when reachable?

### Accessibility and input

- Are structure, names, relationships, status, and errors programmatically available?
- Does keyboard order match visual and task order, with visible focus?
- Do touch targets, contrast, zoom, text resizing, motion preferences, and alternative inputs remain usable?
- Do validation and instructions persist rather than relying on placeholders, color, hover, or memory?
- Do charts, icons, images, audio, and video have appropriate alternatives?

### Responsive and resilient behavior

- Does the composition remain deliberate at critical widths and with long content?
- Do overlays, menus, sticky regions, tables, forms, and actions remain reachable?
- Does the flow survive latency, partial data, repeated items, localization growth, and session expiry?
- Is essential content visible when scripts, observers, fonts, analytics, or decorative media fail?

### Visual system

- Do typography, color, spacing, shape, elevation, imagery, iconography, and motion form one coherent system?
- Does the system fit the surface register: expressive enough for brand, quiet and stable enough for repeated product use?
- Does hierarchy work without decorative containers?
- Are density, contrast, and motion appropriate to the user's physical setting and attention?
- Do components reuse tokens and patterns without flattening every type of content into the same card?

## Marketing and content

### Argument

- Is the audience and situation recognizable?
- Is the promise specific, relevant, differentiated, and proportionate to the proof?
- Does the work explain the mechanism or product role clearly enough?
- Is the main objection addressed without hype or unsupported certainty?
- Is there one clear, appropriately weighted next action?

### Voice and channel

- Is the language concrete, natural, and consistent across headings, body, forms, errors, and actions?
- Does customer language come from named evidence rather than imitation?
- Does the structure match awareness level, traffic source, channel constraints, and device?
- Do adaptations preserve the same promise, proof, offer, and brand voice without copy-pasting the same asset?

### Conversion and measurement

- Does the destination match the acquisition promise?
- Is friction necessary and explained?
- Are variants genuinely different hypotheses rather than synonym swaps?
- Are the intended event, metric, guardrails, window, instrumentation, and decision rule defined?
- Could an apparent result be caused by broken tracking, selection effects, seasonality, or a different journey stage?

## Documents and presentations

- Does the narrative fit the audience, decision, reading setting, and available narration?
- Is information density appropriate per page or slide?
- Are headings, grids, spacing, type, color, and repeated elements consistent?
- Do charts and diagrams communicate real information with units, labels, sources, and honest scales?
- Are images licensed, relevant, and legible rather than decorative filler?
- Does rendered output avoid clipping, overflow, tiny text, awkward breaks, and inaccessible contrast?
- Can the artifact be understood when printed, exported, or viewed without hidden speaker context where required?

## Distinctiveness without novelty theatre

Investigate these as signals, not automatic defects:

- generic hero language and interchangeable section order;
- default category palettes or their equally predictable anti-default replacements;
- repeated icon-card grids, glass panels, pills, oversized radii, glows, or gradient text;
- decorative dashboards, fake data, arbitrary charts, or placeholder proof;
- uniform animation, grain, 3D, illustration, or motion without communicative purpose;
- stock imagery and copy that could be exchanged with a competitor unchanged;
- a visual idea that appears once as decoration rather than operating as a system.

Ask what real product artifact, customer behavior, brand history, physical setting, or content structure should replace the reflex.

## Evidence quality

Prefer direct rendered observation, working behavior, current primary sources, real analytics, and named customer evidence. Mark code-only inspection, synthetic data, undated screenshots, secondary summaries, and inferred intent as weaker evidence. Never upgrade a weak source through confident wording.
